## Chapter 1: Overview and Philosophical Background

This white paper proposes an integrated power system to secure long-term survivability and location infrastructure maintenance for numerical and physical nodes isolated in severe, extreme environments (maritime, polar, desert, space orbit, etc.). Traditional survival systems rely heavily on a single energy source, exhibiting limitations where weather changes or diurnal/seasonal cycle variations in the region lead to power starvation.

Rather than aiming for ultra-high-precision or high-efficiency control, this framework establishes minimization of field maintenance overhead (Low Maintenance) and prevention of node blackout through continuous power charging to maintain indeterminate survival stability as its top priorities.

Inheriting the 'Co-Survival' philosophy of soma-moa and the emergency evacuation guidance principles of the LAST-LIGHT white paper, this system integrates a primary structure (Tier-1) where individual nodes continuously harvest energy native to their extreme environments, and a distributed mesh secondary structure (Tier-2) that mutually senses battery state-of-charge (SOC) among nodes to prioritize power transfer to lower-charged nodes, aiming for energy equalization across the mesh. Furthermore, by incorporating a 'Human-Facing Extension' that wirelessly transmits power to personal devices of evacuees passing near location infrastructure during disasters, the system aims to form a mutual-aid survival infrastructure extending beyond individual power self-sufficiency.

## Chapter 2: Core System Architecture and High-Level Concepts

This system is defined as a high-level architecture linking environmental energy harvesting, node-to-node wireless power sharing, and emergency power supply to human-carried devices.

### 1. Multi-Tier Architecture

 * **Tier 1 (Region-Specific Energy Harvesting Layer)** — Applies harvesting mechanisms optimized for the severe environmental characteristics of each domain to secure primary driving power through passive and continuous charging maintenance rather than strict precision.
 * **Tier 2 (Energy Sharing Mesh Layer)** — Establishes node-to-node wireless power transfer (Laser WPT) channels and applies a Lowest-SOC First Balancing algorithm that prioritizes power allocation to the node with the lowest remaining charge when SOC disparities occur, aligning the average battery level across the mesh.
 * **Human-Facing Extension (Human-Facing Emergency Power Provision Layer)** — Wirelessly supplies minimum survival power to mobile terminals (smartphones, survival wireless devices, etc.) carried by evacuees near infrastructure nodes during disasters and isolation events.

### 2. Inclusive Hardware Definition

In this document, a 'Node' refers to a distributed autonomous survival base station, disaster evacuation guidance anchor, or location guidance device, encompassing terrestrial markers, marine buoys, polar observation sensors, unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs), and low Earth orbit (LEO) satellite modules.

## Chapter 3: Domain-Specific Energy Harvesting Zones

### 1. OCEAN Zone (Maritime Domain)

 * **Primary Energy Source**: Wave dynamics and tidal movement energy
 * **Harvesting Mechanism**: Applies an Oscillating Water Column (OWC) structure integrated with a Wells turbine module. Airflow generated by the vertical motion of the sea surface rotates the turbine in a unidirectional manner to produce continuous electrical power.
 * **Environmental Complementarity**: To mitigate the efficiency degradation of solar panels caused by unstable incident angles due to rotational and dynamic oscillations on marine buoys, wave-driven fluid harvesting is adopted as a primary supplementary power source.

### 2. POLAR Zone (Polar Domain)

 * **Primary Energy Source**: Polar blizzard and extreme wind energy
 * **Harvesting Mechanism**: Features a wind power generation structure integrating small wind turbines with anti-icing heating modules to mitigate icing conditions.
 * **Battery Low-Temperature Discharge Countermeasures**: In polar environments, beyond blade icing, battery cell electrolyte congelation and steep increases in internal resistance due to low temperatures can lead to power depletion. Therefore, a portion of harvested power is recirculated to insulation and thermal self-heating modules to mitigate battery discharge performance degradation.
 * **Bladeless / Piezoelectric Alternative Modules**: Under conditions where severe icing poses a risk of mechanical propeller seizure, alternative installations such as Vortex-Induced Vibration (VIV) bladeless wind turbines, piezoelectric vibration harvesting, or piezoelectric-thermoelectric hybrid harvesting modules can be equipped to mitigate total operational failure.

### 3. DESERT Zone (Desert Domain)

 * **Primary Energy Source**: Thermal energy derived from extreme diurnal temperature differentials
 * **Harvesting Mechanism**: Secures continuous electromotive force through Thermoelectric Generator (TEG) arrays responsive to temperature differentials between the air layer and soil surface layer, or diurnal ambient temperature cycles.

### 4. SPACE Zone (Space/Orbit Domain — Supplementary)

 * **Primary Energy Source**: Interaction with LEO ionospheric plasma and geomagnetic fields
 * **Harvesting Mechanism**: Utilizes Electrodynamic Tether (EDT) conductive cables to generate induced electromotive force through interactions with the ionospheric plasma environment, supplementing auxiliary power and kinetic energy regulation for orbital vehicles.
 * **Future Exploratory Direction**: Lightly suggests the future research possibility of utilizing the electromagnetic drag generated during EDT operation in reverse as a secondary mechanism for deorbiting assistance and deceleration of end-of-life satellites.

## Chapter 4: Common Layer — Distributed Energy Sharing Mesh and Human-Facing Extension

### 1. Node-to-Node Wireless Power Transfer and Lowest-SOC Priority Balancing

P2P energy sharing among terrestrial, marine, aerial, and orbital nodes is executed based on directional Laser Wireless Power Transfer (Laser WPT) technology.

 * **Energy Redistribution Threshold Criteria (Linked with soma-moa L2 FSM)**: When a node's battery state-of-charge (SOC) reaches 80% or above (PRELOCK 80% pattern), that node is defined as being in a 'Surplus Power Confirmed State', transferring power to 'Urgent Charge Demand Nodes' whose SOC has dropped to 20% or below, or to the lowest-charged node in the mesh.
 * **Lowest-SOC Priority Balancing**: Periodically shares SOC states among nodes in the mesh and prioritizes power allocation to the node with the lowest charge level, promoting energy level equalization across the entire mesh.
 * **Fine Pointing and Tracking Limitations Notice**: Mechanisms for fine pointing and tracking under dynamic marine motion or UAV mobility conditions are not claimed as fully verified mechanical implementations within this white paper, and are explicitly stated as precision engineering challenges to be resolved in advanced optical and servo control domains.

### 2. Human-Facing Emergency Wireless Power Auxiliary Layer (Human-Facing Extension)

Interlinked with the disaster evacuation guidance framework of the LAST-LIGHT white paper, this layer redistributes power to personal survival devices carried by evacuees when passing near anchor infrastructure nodes.

 * **Human-Safe Mandatory Wavelength Band (1550nm Eye-Safe IR)**: To fundamentally prevent damage to the eyes and skin of evacuees during medium-range wireless power transfer, infrared (IR) light sources in the 1550nm wavelength band—which exhibit extremely low ocular crystalline lens transmittance and established human safety—are specified as a mandatory requirement.
 * **Near-Field Magnetic Resonance Power Transfer**: Transfers emergency power via magnetic resonance within contact or near-field range (within several tens of centimeters) of evacuation guidance anchors, relaxing device orientation and positioning constraints.
 * **Quality of Service Priority Concept (QoS Priority Concept)**: When multiple mobile devices connect simultaneously in disaster areas, an upper-layer QoS priority scheme is applied to allocate power preferentially to terminals executing minimum survival communications, such as transmitting emergency SOS signals and GPS location coordinates, over general charging.
 * **Minimum Survival Power Expectation**: Considering the antenna and light-receptive limitations of compact handheld devices, providing auxiliary power to maintain minimum survival communications—rather than full battery replenishment—is established as the realistic objective.

### 3. Weather Attenuation and Unverified Combination Notice

 * **Weather Attenuation Conditions**: Under atmospheric transmittance degradation conditions such as dense fog, heavy snowfall, blizzards, and sea fog, laser and optical transmission efficiency may be significantly attenuated, which is explicitly noted as an operational limit condition (AS-IS).
 * **Unverified Exploratory Model Notice**: The overall combined framework of integrating individual energy harvesting nodes via laser WPT and infrared/magnetic resonance mesh networks to share power between infrastructure and human devices represents an exploratory proposal requiring ongoing empirical investigation.

## Chapter 5: Mathematical Models and Performance Mitigation Mechanisms

### 1. DESERT Zone Thermoelectric Performance Temperature Attenuation Model

When ambient temperature fluctuations deviate from the optimal operating temperature differential of thermoelectric elements, the efficiency attenuation of thermoelectric power generation can be defined by the following equation:

$$\beta(T_{curr}) = \beta_0 \cdot \left(1 - \frac{|T_{curr} - T_{opt}|}{T_{crit}}\right)$$

 * $\beta(T_{curr})$: Power generation efficiency correction factor at current effective element temperature $T_{curr}$
 * $\beta_0$: Baseline power generation efficiency under optimal operating conditions
 * $T_{opt}$: Optimal design operating temperature of the thermoelectric module
 * $T_{crit}$: Critical upper-limit temperature differential for power generation ($\beta(T_{curr}) = 0$ is imposed where $|T_{curr} - T_{opt}| \ge T_{crit}$)
 * **Estimation Notice**: Benchmark example values attached to this model for critical temperature differential (e.g., $T_{crit} = 80^\circ\text{C} \sim 100^\circ\text{C}$) represent target benchmark values for theoretical understanding and are estimated figures prior to empirical measurement (AS-IS Target Benchmark).

### 2. Wireless Power Transfer Reception Model Reflecting Atmospheric Dissipation

The net power received at transmission distance $d$ under atmospheric transmittance conditions is modeled by the following relaxation equation:

$$P_{rx} = P_{tx} \cdot \eta_{tx} \cdot \eta_{rx} \cdot e^{-\gamma \cdot d}$$

 * $P_{rx}$: Net effective power reaching the photovoltaic conversion unit of the receiving node or handheld device
 * $P_{tx}$: Output power of the transmitting node laser/infrared module
 * $\eta_{tx}$: Conversion efficiency of the transmitting element
 * $\eta_{rx}$: Photovoltaic/power conversion efficiency of the receiving element
 * $\gamma$: Atmospheric dissipation/attenuation coefficient determined by weather conditions (fog, snowfall, sea fog, etc.)
 * $d$: Wireless power transmission distance between nodes or between node and human device
 * **Estimation Notice**: Numerical examples of atmospheric dissipation coefficient $\gamma$ across weather conditions (clear sky, dense fog, etc.) are designated as target estimated benchmark figures prior to empirical measurement (AS-IS Target Benchmark).

### 3. Lowest-SOC Priority Balancing Weight Model

When transmitting surplus power across the mesh network to a specific receiving node, the priority reception weight $W_i$ for equalizing battery levels among nodes is calculated by the following conditional equation:

$$W_i = \max(0,\ \bar{S} - S_i)$$

 * $W_i$: Power reception priority weight for the $i$-th node (larger values indicate higher priority to receive power)
 * $\bar{S}$: Average battery state-of-charge (SOC) among currently active nodes in the mesh network
 * $S_i$: Current battery state-of-charge (SOC) of the $i$-th node
 * A node whose current SOC $S_i$ is lower than the mesh average $\bar{S}$ yields a larger weight $W_i$, concentrating power delivery to the lowest-charged nodes to promote energy equalization across the mesh.
